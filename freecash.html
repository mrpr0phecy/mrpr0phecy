A get-paid-to site that pays real money for surveys, game installs and app trials. Whether that is worth your time depends entirely on what your time is worth — so here are the actual numbers rather than the usual "earn £500 a month from your sofa" nonsense.

What it actually is

Freecash is a "GPT" (get-paid-to) platform. Advertisers pay it to put real people in front of surveys, mobile games and app trials. It keeps a cut and passes some to you. You cash out via PayPal, gift cards, bank transfer or crypto once you clear the minimum.

The business model is honest enough and it is not hidden: you are the product. Your attention and your survey answers are the thing being sold. That is a fair trade if you go in knowing it, and a bad surprise if you don't.

Worth a look if…

Skip it if…

  • You could work an extra paid hour instead. That will beat a day of surveys, near enough always.
  • You're in financial difficulty. This will not move the needle, and the time is better spent on MoneyHelper or Citizens Advice — both free and impartial.
  • You're outside the supported countries — rewards are far thinner or absent.
  • You hate giving out personal data. Surveys profile you; that's the entire point of them.

The catches, before you sign up

  1. Survey screen-outs are unpaid. You can spend five minutes answering qualifying questions and be rejected for not matching the target demographic. This is normal across the whole industry, and it is the single biggest drag on the real hourly rate.
  2. Big game offers are long. An offer paying £40 might require reaching a level that takes two weeks of daily play. The pay is real; the time cost is easy to underestimate. Read the requirement fully before you start, not after.
  3. Free trials that need card details. Some offers ask you to start a paid subscription's free trial. If you forget to cancel, you pay more than you earned. Set a phone reminder the moment you sign up, or skip those offers entirely.
  4. Cash-out minimums and verification. You need to reach a threshold before withdrawing, and payouts go through verification checks. Legitimate, but it means the money is not instant.
  5. It's taxable. Money earned here counts toward your £1,000 trading allowance. Under that in a tax year and there's generally nothing to report; over it — combined across all your side income, not per activity — and you must register for Self Assessment. Most casual users will never come close, but it's your responsibility, not theirs.
