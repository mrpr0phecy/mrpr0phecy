For Dew

Twenty-two poems for Dew, written in the dark before morning. Some are about water. Most are about you. One page is locked, and the key is one word.

Dew —

I could have bought flowers. Flowers are sensible: an afternoon, a card, done.

Instead there is this: a garden made of light, twenty-two poems, three small mysteries, and a last page that opens with your name. There is a reasonable amount of physics pretending to be romantic, and one section where I am almost honest about myself.

Take your time with it. There is no timer. It is a website; it will wait.

iii

Sonnet at Six

sonnet · 14 lines

The lawn is holding light it cannot keep;the hedge stands silver, not yet green or grey;a blackbird wakes the street from half-sleepand finds the morning not quite on its way.I have been up since four, and cannot saywhat kept me — only that the sky went deepand something in the quiet made me stayto watch the ordinary learn to leap.

You'll ask me, later, what I did all night.I'll say: I made a small machine of glassthat holds a drop of light and holds it right,and taught it how to turn as hours pass.

You'll laugh and call it strange. It's true. And yetthe drop is just the morning's alphabet.

fourteen lines, because you once said short things feel unfinished

Instructions for the Morning

imperative

1.Wake before the kettle.The grass has been working all nightand would like a witness.

2.Walk the long way round.The shortest path is for peoplewho have already seen everything.

3.Do not wipe the windscreenwith your sleeve. That greyis the sky, resting.

4.When you find a beadbalanced on the edge of a leaf,leave it. It is holdingmore of the morning than you are.

5.If someone asks why you're late,tell them the truth:the light kept stopping to be looked at.

mostly advice to myself

The Weight of a Drop

lyric

A drop weighs almost nothing.That is the trick of it:almost.

It can carry a whole roof,a telephone wire, a stranger's window,the shape of the hour before work —

and still let gothe moment the sunasks it to.

for the ones who hold everything and say nothing about it

x

The Physicist's Love Poem

free verse

Surface tension, they call it:the way water prefersits own company so muchit will wear a skinrather than touch the world.

I understand that.I have been a person who preferredhis own company in a closed room,with a screen and a problem,for eleven hours.

Then you sat down.

Refraction is the other one:light, arriving late and slow,bending toward the denser thing.

I looked it up. It is not a choice.Light does not decideto lean toward what is heavier.

It simply arrives,finds the world suddenly fuller,and bends.

yes, I did check the definition. twice

Ways of Looking at Dew

five small rooms

i.Among twenty-one windowsthe only moving thingwas the bead of dew.

ii.I was of three minds,like a leafthat is holding three dropsand afraid of arithmetic.

iii.The garden turnedin the small wind.The dew did not.It had already decided.

iv.Once, the morning said,the drop is only water.The drop said nothing,and went on holding the sky.

v.It was morning all day.It was going to be morningall day. The dewhad already gone backto being an argument with gravity.

an apology to Wallace Stevens, who did it better with a blackbird

xiv

Field Guide to Her

notes toward a species

Habitat. Anywhere with a window, a kettle, or a cat that has decided she is staff.

Voice. Low in the morning, quick by ten, dangerous after a second coffee.

Diet. Tea. Other people's leftovers. The last ten minutes of any film.

Behaviour. Will not walk past a dog without speaking to it. Laughs before the joke lands. Claims not to be brave, then says the true thing in a room full of people who were avoiding it. Reads the last page first, then denies it. Remembers everyone's drink and nobody's birthday.

Conservation. Least concerned. Which is the problem. Someone should be concerned. I am concerned. Consider this the concern.

Notes for observers. Do not mistake quiet for agreement. The key is under the mat, and she has known all along.

compiled from evidence, not hope

xx

Nothing Was Asked of the Grass

villanelle · 19 lines

Nothing was asked of the grass.It gave its silver back to the airand let the morning pass.

The gate leans grey; the hour is glass;the kettle sighs, the chairs are bare.Nothing was asked of the grass.

I know a man who let the grasskeep what it held, and did not careto make the morning pass.

He'd rather watch a leaf's edge glasshold one bright square of morning therethan say it. Nothing was asked of the grass.

And when the sun begins to massalong the roofs and everywhereis morning — let it pass.

So here's the page, the dew, the glass,the gate, the leaning hour, the air:nothing was asked of the grass.It let the morning pass.

the form is stubborn. so am I

Seven Sealed Secrets

turn them over · the order is not random · nor are the first letters

Seven small admissions about the man who made this page. He was asked to explain, tastefully, why he is worth the trouble. He has delegated the job to a series of sealed cards, which is either modesty or cowardice. Open them in order. The first letters of the seven are hiding a word — and it is a word you will be using shortly anyway.

Field Notes on the Author

seven observations · kept by nobody in particular

He refused to write this section himself, on the grounds that a man listing his own virtues is a man you should edge away from at a party. So these are observations, in the third person, which is the only way he could be persuaded. Read the first letters downwards: seven notes, one word, and it is his one good quality.

Note i

Patient in the way that looks like stubbornness: he will give an entire evening to a two-degree rotation that nobody will ever notice, and call it a good evening.

Note ii

Accurate to the point of being annoying. He will tell you the thing is forty-seven pixels out of place. It is forty-seven pixels out of place.

Note iii

Terrible at leaving a thing half-finished. The locked page at the end of this is his idea of a joke, and also his idea of a hand held out.

Note iv

Interested. Ask him about surface tension at one in the morning and cancel the rest of your evening; bring a chair. He will be delighted, and he will draw diagrams, and you will understand them.

Note v

Embarrassingly easy to please. One laugh from you and the whole week is accounted for. He will deny that this is a weakness.

Note vi

Not, in fact, arrogant — but he did ask for a section explaining why he is worth the trouble, which is its own kind of evidence, and he knows it, and that is why it is written down here instead of by him.

Note vii

Trying, quietly, all the time, at things that don't look like effort from the outside. That is the trick of him: it always looks easy afterwards. It is never easy at the time.

The Locked Page

One word opens it.

It is one syllable. It is on the cover of this page. It is in the first letters of part three, and in the first letters of the seven secrets, and in the seventh riddle. It is also your name — which makes this the easiest lock ever built. The point was never the lock.
