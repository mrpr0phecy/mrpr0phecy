🕵️ Benford's Law First-Digit Scanner

In real-world data — expenses, populations, invoices — a leading 1 shows up about 30% of the time and a 9 under 5%. Paste a column of numbers and this scanner compares their first digits with the log₁₀(1 + 1/d) curve, computes χ², the mean absolute deviation and a Nigrini-style conformity verdict used by fraud auditors.

Paste any text that contains numbers — one per line, comma-separated, a CSV column, even a whole report. Every number with a non-zero leading digit counts; nothing leaves your browser.

Benford's law holds when data spans several orders of magnitude and isn't bounded (invoice totals, town populations, river lengths). It fails for uniform or bounded things like ages or lottery numbers — check the data type before believing the verdict. A flag means “look closer”, never “guilty”.
