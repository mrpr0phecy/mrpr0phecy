How to use it, the keyboard, mirror mode and reading tips

Getting a natural read. Paste the script, then press Match speed to my pace: it measures the word count and sets the scroll speed so the whole script takes as long as it would at your chosen words-per-minute. Most people speak at 130–160 wpm on camera; slower reads sound more confident, so start at "Natural" and slow down if you find yourself racing the text. The eye-line guide marks where you should be reading — keep it near the top of the screen and close to the lens, so your eyes barely move. Use a bigger text size than feels necessary; if you can read it without squinting from twice the distance, it is right.

Keyboard and touch. Space plays and pauses. ↑/↓ change speed in small steps, ← jumps back ten seconds, Home returns to the top, F toggles full screen and Esc closes. On a phone or tablet, tap the text to pause and resume, and drag to scrub — the same keys work from a Bluetooth keyboard, presentation clicker or a foot pedal that sends Page Down/Up (mapped to speed). A phone on a tripod beside the camera makes a perfectly good prompter.

Mirror mode. A beam-splitter prompter reflects the screen in a piece of glass in front of the lens, which reverses the image. Tick Mirror horizontally so the reflection reads correctly; some rigs also need Flip vertically. The controls stay the right way round.

Writing for the prompter. Short sentences, one idea per paragraph and a blank line between them. Write numbers as you would say them ("two point five million", not 2,500,000). Put [pause] where you want to breathe — it is shown as a marker and left out of the timing. Your script and settings are stored in this browser only, so a refresh does not lose them and nothing is uploaded.
