How tiling works, printing settings that matter, and assembling the poster

How it works. Pick how many pages wide the poster should be and the maker scales your picture to that width; the height follows from the picture’s own proportions, so a landscape photo three pages wide might be two pages tall. Each page carries its own slice of the image plus a strip of overlap along the right-hand and bottom edges. When you assemble, each page goes over the overlap strip of its neighbour, which hides the join and forgives the millimetre or two that home printers wander by. Fine cut marks show exactly where the real edge of each tile is, and a small label in the margin (A1, A2, B1…) names the row and column. The first page is an assembly map: a thumbnail of the whole poster with the grid and labels drawn over it.

Printer settings that matter. Print at 100% / actual size, never “fit to page” — scaling would shrink every tile by a different amount and the joins would not meet. Most inkjets cannot print to the very edge, which is what the printer margin is for: 10 mm is safe for nearly everything, 5 mm works if your printer supports borderless or near-borderless printing. Heavier paper (120 gsm or more) is easier to glue and hangs flatter. The black & white option turns the picture to greyscale before it is drawn so a colour printer uses only the black cartridge.

Assembling. Trim the left and top margin off every page except those in the first column and first row, using the cut marks as your guide. Lay out row A left to right, sticking each page over the overlap of the one before with a glue stick or double-sided tape; then start row B under it. A long ruler and a craft knife give cleaner edges than scissors. For a text banner choose one letter per page and the letters can simply be hung side by side on a string.

Quality. The pages are drawn at 150 dots per inch, which is plenty for a poster viewed from a metre away; the limiting factor is your source image. The preview reports the effective resolution, and anything above about 40 pixels per centimetre looks crisp. Nothing you upload leaves your device — the whole thing runs in this tab.
