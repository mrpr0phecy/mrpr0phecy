Exhibit A · The living beam

Load a beam and watch it flex

What you're seeing. The top beam shows the deflection curve — how much it bends. The middle diagram is shear force (V) — the internal cutting force. The bottom diagram is bending moment (M) — the internal twisting force. The stress colour map on the beam shows where tension (bottom, blue) and compression (top, red) are greatest. When stress exceeds the material's strength, the beam fails.
Exhibit B · Why beams are tall, not wide

The moment of inertia secret

A beam's resistance to bending depends on its moment of inertia (I = bd³/12). Notice the d³ — depth is cubed. Double the depth and you get 8× stronger. This is why I-beams are tall and thin, why floor joists are placed on edge, and why a ruler bends easily flat but is stiff on its side.
