Why linear spice scaling ruins big batches — and the 78.4 °C alcohol rule

Sub-linear N0.75 whole-spice scaling: If a 1-bottle mulled wine recipe uses 5 whole cloves and you multiply linearly by 8 bottles for a party, 40 cloves release so much eugenol during a 2-hour hold that the pot tastes medicinal and bitter. Professional batch bartenders scale whole aromatics (cloves, star anise, cinnamon quills) by (V / V₀)^0.75 and pull the spice bag after 35–40 minutes of steeping.

The syrup-base trick & 78.4 °C ceiling: Pure ethanol boils at 78.4 °C at sea level, and aqueous wine mixtures begin losing volatile aroma esters and alcohol vapour rapidly once a pot simmers above 80 °C. Instead of boiling the wine, simmer just the citrus peel, sugar, spices and a splash of liquid into a concentrated spiced syrup for 8 minutes first; then pour in the main bottles and hold gently at 68–74 °C (the "Keep Warm" setting on a slow cooker). Always provide a clearly labelled 0% spiced orchard cider pot alongside alcoholic punches for drivers and non-drinking guests.
