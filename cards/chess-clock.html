Keyboard: Q A Z or left Shift press the top/left clock, P L M or right Shift the bottom/right one; Enter presses whichever is running; Space pauses and resumes.

How the modes work, and how to use it at the board

Using it. Put the phone between you, or the laptop where both players can reach it. The player who moves second taps their own side to start the first player's clock (in chess, Black starts White's clock). After that, each player taps their own side when their move is complete: their clock stops and the opponent's starts. A tap on the side that is not running does nothing, so a stray finger cannot give away time. The pause button in the middle freezes both clocks — tap it again, or the running player's side, to resume. Reset asks twice. Full screen turns the whole display into the two clocks, and the top clock is rotated so the opponent reads it the right way up; untick Rotate for side-by-side play on a laptop. With Keep the screen awake on, a phone will not lock mid-game in browsers that support it.

Fischer increment (the "+2" in "3+2") adds the increment to your clock each time you complete a move — it is the standard in online and most over-the-board chess. Simple delay, the United States Chess Federation standard, waits the delay time before your clock starts counting down on each move, so fast moves cost nothing but time never accumulates. Bronstein delay counts down immediately but gives back what you used, up to the delay, when you press — mathematically the same as simple delay but the clock visibly moves. Byo-yomi is the Go convention: once your main time is gone you get a number of periods (say five of 30 seconds); a move made within a period resets it to the full length, and a period that runs out is lost for good. Your flag falls when the last one runs out. The side shows how many periods remain.

Time odds lets a stronger player start with less time — tick the box and give the second player their own main time. The increment or delay is shared. Changes to the time control apply immediately if the game has not started, and on the next reset otherwise, so nobody can adjust a running clock.

Accuracy. Time is measured from the browser's high-resolution clock, not by counting ticks, so a slow phone or a background tab cannot make it drift: when the tab returns, the running clock has advanced by exactly the time that passed. Seconds are shown rounded up as physical clocks do (5:00 stays until a full second has gone), and tenths appear under ten seconds. The last ten seconds tick if sounds are on; a falling flag beeps three times. Nothing is sent anywhere; your settings are kept on this device only.
