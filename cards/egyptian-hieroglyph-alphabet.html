𓂀 The Uniliteral Case

The twenty-four single-consonant signs every student copies first — Gardiner’s teaching alphabet. Vowels were not written. Type a name; the case sets the glyphs and the catalogue codes. This is not a translation of a stela.

Curator’s note. Conventional ASCII: A = ꜣ (aleph), a = ꜥ (ayin), H = ḥ, x = ḫ, X = ẖ, S = š, T = ṯ, D = ḏ, q = q. Spaces and unknown letters fall through as gaps. Direction here is left-to-right for the screen; stone more often reads toward the faces of the signs.

Sign list: Gardiner, Egyptian Grammar (1957), uniliterals. Unicode Egyptian Hieroglyphs (U+13000) — if squares appear, the font on this device lacks the block; the Gardiner codes on the plaque still identify the exhibit.
