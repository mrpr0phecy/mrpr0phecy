Which type to pick, how the sheets are laid out, and printing tips

Badge inserts are 2⅓ × 3⅜ in (about 59 × 86 mm), the size that slides into the common clip-on and lanyard badge holders, eight to a sheet in two columns. Print on plain card, cut on the marks and slot them in. “Hello my name is” stickers use the same eight-up grid with the classic band; print on full-sheet label paper and cut, or on plain paper and use tape. Tent cards are folded in half so the name stands up on a table: each card is printed twice, once upside-down, so it reads correctly from both sides once folded along the dotted line — two tents per sheet, ideal for panels and workshops. Small place cards work the same way at dinner-setting size, six per sheet. Address-size labels match the ubiquitous 30-per-sheet layout (1 × 2⅝ in), handy for name labels on coats, lunch bags or storage boxes.

Long names. The name is scaled to fit its card, so “Dr Alexandra Konstantinopoulou” simply prints smaller rather than overflowing. Add a role, table number or pronouns after a “|” and it appears as a second, smaller line. Names are used exactly as typed, so check capitalisation before you print 200 of them.

Printing. Download the PDF and print at 100% (“actual size”, not “fit to page”) so the cards match the holders. The layout is centred on the page with equal margins, so a small printer offset will not push anything off the sheet. Card stock of 200–250 gsm stands up well as a tent; ordinary 80 gsm paper will flop. For stickers, the cut marks sit in the gutters so a slightly wandering scissor cut stays outside the band. Nothing you type is sent anywhere; the sheets are drawn and the PDF is assembled in your browser.
