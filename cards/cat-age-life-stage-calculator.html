Life stages, the conversion table and what each stage needs

The conversion. The first cat year is roughly 15 human years, the second adds about 9 (24 at age two), and each year after that adds about 4. So a 10-year-old cat is around 56 and a 20-year-old around 96. Charts differ by a year or two — treat it as a rough translation, not a biological measurement.

The life stages (2021 AAHA/AAFP Feline Life Stage Guidelines). Kitten: birth to 1 year · young adult: 1–6 years · mature adult: 7–10 years · senior: over 10 years, plus an end-of-life stage that can come at any age. The guidelines call for at least an annual examination for every cat, and at least every six months for senior cats.

Kitten milestones in the UK. First core vaccination at 8–9 weeks and the second at 12 weeks (a third at 16 weeks in some risk areas), neutering commonly from 4 months, and — in England, since 10 June 2024 — every pet cat must be microchipped and registered before it is 20 weeks old, with a fine of up to £500 for owners who do not comply within 21 days. The socialisation window closes at about 9 weeks, which is why early, gentle handling matters so much.

What vets watch as cats age. From about 7: kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, high blood pressure, dental disease and arthritis — all common, all quiet at the start, which is why baseline bloods, urine and a blood-pressure check are worth asking for. Senior cats often hide weight loss under a thicker coat: weigh at home monthly and compare.
