Your rights, at a glance

UK — stopped or in custody

  • You do not have to answer questions — but in the UK, silence after a caution can be used in limited ways, so get a solicitor before deciding.
  • Free legal advice in custody — a duty solicitor, before and during any interview, in most cases.
  • You may promptly tell one person you are detained — a relative, friend or employer.
  • Bail is the default. Custody before trial is the exception, and you can apply for bail again.
  • At a stop and search the officer must show their details, record the search, and give you a receipt on request.
  • The burden of proof is always on the prosecution. You never have to prove your innocence.

US — stopped or in custody

  • Right to remain silent — say "I'm invoking my right to remain silent" and questioning must stop.
  • Right to an attorney — say "I want a lawyer" and questioning must stop until one is present.
  • You can decline a consent search — "no, officer" is a legal answer, not an offence.
  • Bail is a right in most cases (no excessive bail); you can ask the judge to reduce or waive it.
  • For serious offences you have the right to a jury trial.
  • You may ask "am I free to go?" — if the answer is yes, walk away calmly.

Not legal advice. This is plain-English information, not a substitute for a lawyer — laws change, and US states differ from one another. If your liberty, money or record is at stake, get proper advice: in the UK, Citizens Advice, a solicitor or LawWorks; in the US, a public defender or your state's legal-aid office. In an emergency, call 999 (UK) or 911 (US) first.
