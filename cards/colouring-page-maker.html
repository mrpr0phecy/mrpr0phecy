Getting good outlines, and how the conversion works

Pick the right photo. The best colouring pages come from pictures with a clear subject on a plain background, in even light: a pet on a sofa, a child against a wall, a car, a building. Busy backgrounds (foliage, gravel, patterned fabric) turn into a mess of scribbles; a quick crop before you start helps more than any slider. Strong shadows become black blobs, so shoot in shade or on an overcast day if you can.

The sliders. Detail sets how fine a change in brightness counts as an edge: low values keep only the big shapes (good for toddlers and thick crayons), high values pick up fur, hair and texture (better for older kids or adults with fine pens). Line weight thickens every line after detection, which makes pages easier to colour inside and prints more reliably on cheap printers. Remove specks deletes isolated dots and tiny fragments that are not connected to anything larger.

How it works. The photo’s contrast is normalised and each colour channel is blurred by an amount set by the detail slider so that texture finer than that is ignored. The Canny edge detector then measures how quickly colour changes at every pixel (red, green and blue are combined, so a yellow sun on a blue sky still has an edge even where the brightness happens to match), thins each edge to a single pixel along the direction of change, and keeps faint edges only where they connect to strong ones — which is why the result is made of continuous contours rather than the speckle a simple threshold gives. Lines are then thickened to your chosen weight and any connected fragment shorter than a couple of dozen pixels is dropped. The pencil sketch uses a different trick: the greyscale image is divided by a blurred, inverted copy of itself (a “colour dodge” blend), which lifts flat areas to white and leaves soft graphite-like shading only where tone changes quickly. Bold cartoon outlines run the line method at a lower detail setting with heavy thickening.

Printing. The PDF places the drawing on a full page with a small margin, portrait or landscape to match the picture, with your title at the top. Print at 100% (not “fit to page”) for the intended line weight. Everything runs locally in your browser, so a photo of your children never leaves your device.
