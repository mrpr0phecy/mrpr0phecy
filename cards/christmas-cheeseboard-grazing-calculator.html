How monger portioning, flavour progression & tempering rules work

Portion sizing by meal context: Professional cheesemongers size post-roast dessert boards at 70–80 g per guest (diners are already full from turkey and pudding), cocktail and evening grazing platters at 125–135 g per guest, and standalone Twixmas supper spreads (paired with crusty sourdough and charcuterie) at 190–210 g per guest.

Crowd consumption skew: Guests never eat cheeses equally. Milder aged hard cheeses (aged West Country Cheddar, Comté, Gruyère) and creamy bloomy rinds (Brie de Meaux, Camembert, Tunworth) account for roughly 60–65% of total bites, while pungent blue cheeses (Colston Bassett Stilton, Roquefort) andfresh goat logs move more slowly unless the room specifically loves blue. Rounding each wedge up to the nearest 25 g prevents sad fingernail-sized scraps.

The 60-minute tempering rule & pasteurised note: Cold fridge fat (`4 °C`) mutes volatile aroma compounds and makes Brie chalky. Pull cheeses out of the fridge 60 minutes before serving, covered loosely under wax paper so cut faces do not sweat or dry out. Give blue and soft cheeses dedicated knives so Penicillium roqueforti mould spores do not cross onto mild cheeses. If catering for pregnant or immunocompromised guests, clearly label unpasteurised or mould-ripened soft/blue cheeses per NHS food guidance.
