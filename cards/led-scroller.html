🚦 LED Scroller — Big Scrolling Text Sign for Your Phone Screen

Type a message and your screen becomes a full-screen LED sign: scroll it, hold it static, or flash it slowly, in dot-matrix red, amber, green or rainbow. For airport pickups, concerts, protests, taxis and saying hi across a room.

Tap the sign to see controls · Esc closes · full screen hides the address bar.

Tips for being seen, and how the sign is drawn

Be seen. Turn the screen brightness all the way up, use full screen so the browser bar goes away, and hold the phone sideways — the text scales to the short side of the screen, so landscape gives letters about twice the height. In a crowd, scrolling text is read from further away than static text because motion catches the eye; up close a static sign is easier. Red and amber LEDs read best in dark venues, white and cyan in daylight. Turn on Rotate 90° if you would rather hold the phone upright: the message runs top to bottom and you read it by tilting your head, which is how people actually hold a phone above their head at a gig.

Keep it short. Three to six words. A scrolling sign shows one word-ish at a time, so a long sentence takes a while to come round again; if you need more, raise the speed or shrink the text height so more letters fit on screen at once. Capital letters are the norm on real signs because their even height lights more dots; turn them off for names that should keep their spelling.

Flashing. The flash mode blinks once a second, on purpose. Anything faster than three flashes per second is a photosensitive-seizure risk and is the limit in the web accessibility guidelines, so there is no strobe setting. If you want attention, scrolling does the job without the risk.

How it is drawn. The message is rendered once into a tiny off-screen bitmap only as tall as the number of LED rows you picked (11, 16 or 24). Every frame, each lit pixel in that bitmap is drawn as a glowing dot on a dark grid; the unlit dots stay faintly visible, the way a real dot-matrix board looks. Scrolling moves the text a whole dot column at a time, so it steps exactly like a shop sign rather than sliding smoothly. Smooth bold text skips all that and draws the message in a heavy sans-serif, which is easier to read at a distance in bright light. Everything runs on your phone; the Copy link button puts the message and settings in the address, so you can send someone a sign they can open and hold up straight away.
