National Insurance — Class 1 employee (2026/27):
• £12,570–£50,270: 8%
• Over £50,270: 2%
• Under £12,570: 0%
The £100,000 trap (this calculator models it):
Above £100,000 the personal allowance is withdrawn by £1 for every £2 earned, and is gone entirely at £125,140. Between those two points every extra £100 of salary is taxed at an effective 60% — 62% once the 2% National Insurance is added. It is the highest marginal rate in the UK system, and it is higher than the 45% additional rate that sits above it.
A pension contribution is the usual lever: it reduces adjusted net income, and inside the taper it buys back allowance at roughly twice the headline relief.
Scotland — six bands, set at Holyrood (2026/27):
• £12,571–£16,537: 19% starter
• £16,538–£29,526: 20% basic
• £29,527–£43,662: 21% intermediate
• £43,663–£75,000: 42% higher
• £75,001–£125,140: 45% advanced
• Over £125,140: 48% top

The crossover is around £33,500: below it a Scottish taxpayer pays slightly less than an English one, above it progressively more. The 42% band starting at £43,663 rather than £50,270 is the single biggest driver — that £6,600 slice is taxed at 20% in England and 42% in Scotland, a 50% marginal rate once National Insurance is added. In the £100k taper zone the Scottish effective marginal rate reaches roughly 67.5% against 60% in England.

The personal allowance, its taper and National Insurance are all UK-wide and identical either side of the border.
