🌊 Tsunami Wave Tank

Nonlinear shallow-water tank. An earthquake lifts a patch of seafloor in deep water; the resulting long wave races at √(gh), shoals and steepens on the shelf, then runs up the beach. Change depth, slope and slip — Green's law is not a slogan here, it is what the grid does.

Press Quake (or click the deep ocean) to displace the seafloor.
The physics

1-D nonlinear shallow-water equations on a staggered grid: ∂η/∂t + ∂[(h+η)u]/∂x = 0 and ∂u/∂t + u∂u/∂x + g∂η/∂x + r u|u|/(h+η) = 0. Deep-water celerity is √(gh) — about 200 m/s in 4 km of water, so a Pacific crossing takes hours, not minutes. As the wave climbs the shelf, Green's law (η ∝ h−1/4) amplifies height while the front steepens into a bore. Run-up is the highest dry-bed elevation the wet/dry front reaches.
