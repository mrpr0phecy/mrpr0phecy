How cone surface area, bauble ratios & LED wattage are worked out

Why bottom branches eat twice the lights of the top

A Christmas tree is a cone whose lateral surface area grows with radius × slant height. The bottom third of a 6.5 ft tree carries roughly 55 % of the total branch area, the middle third 33 %, and the top crown just 12 %. Wrap from the trunk outward on the lower tiers first, or you will run out of cable with a bare skirt.

The 50 / 35 / 15 professional bauble split

Stylists balance visual depth by hanging 15 % large statement ornaments (8–10 cm) nestled slightly deeper inside branches first, 35 % medium baubles (6–7 cm) mid-branch, and 50 % small/accent baubles (3–5 cm) near the tips to catch the LED reflections.

Electricity cost check

Modern LED fairy lights draw roughly 4–6 watts per 100 bulbs (often less with transformer efficiency included). Even 1,600 LEDs running 7 hours a night for 32 days uses roughly 18 kWh — under £4.50 for the entire festive season at 24.5 p/kWh, versus ~£37 for vintage filament bulbs.
