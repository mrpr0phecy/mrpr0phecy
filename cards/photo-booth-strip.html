How the booth works, printing the strip, and privacy

How it works. Start the camera, pick a filter (the live view previews it), then press Take the photos. A 3-2-1 countdown runs before each shot, the screen flashes and a shutter clicks — the classic booth rhythm, so everyone has time to change pose between frames. Each photo is captured at the camera’s full resolution, cropped to 4:3, and dropped into the strip on the right. Not happy with one frame? Tap it and choose Retake to replace just that shot; the others stay. No camera, or want to use photos you already took? Use photos from my device fills the frames from your gallery instead.

The strip. The classic layout is a 2×6 inch strip at 300 dpi (1200×3600 pixels), the size photo booths have printed since the 1950s, so it comes out crisp from any photo printer or print shop — order a 2×6 print or print two side by side on 4×6 paper and cut down the middle. The square grid layout suits phone wallpapers and social posts. Choose the paper colour, add a caption and the date, and download a PNG or copy the image to paste into a message.

Filters are applied to the pixels themselves when a shot is taken, so the download matches the preview: Black & white and Sepia are the booth classics, Vintage adds fade and a soft vignette, Film noir pushes contrast, Warm and Cool shift the colour cast, Pop lifts saturation and contrast. Change the filter after shooting and the strip re-renders from the original frames, so nothing is baked in until you download. Mirror keeps each photo the way you saw yourself in the preview, which is what a booth does; turn it off if there is writing in the shot.

Privacy. The camera feed goes straight from your webcam to this page and is never recorded, uploaded or stored; the shots live in your browser’s memory only and vanish when you leave or press Start over. The camera light goes off as soon as you stop the camera or navigate away. Your caption and settings are remembered locally so a refresh does not reset them.
