Where the times come from — and the peeling tricks that actually work

Method. The baseline is the widely used test-kitchen figure for a large fridge-cold egg lowered into boiling water: about 6½ minutes for soft, 7–8 for jammy, 11 for hard. Medium and extra-large eggs shift that by roughly ¾–1 minute (a gram of egg is a gram of egg — bigger simply heats through more slowly), room-temperature eggs need about ¾ minute less, and a cold-water start adds time for the pan to return to the boil, which is why that method is more variable between stoves.

Altitude. Water boils about 1 °C cooler for every 285 m you climb, so at 1,000 m it barely reaches 97 °C and eggs set more slowly. The estimate adds roughly 7% of time per 1,000 m — treat it as a starting point, do a test egg, and adjust. Above 3,000 m expect to experiment.

Peeling. Three things genuinely make eggs peel cleanly: use eggs a week or more old (very fresh whites cling to the shell membrane), dunk them in iced water for at least 2 minutes so the egg contracts away from the shell, and peel under a trickle of water starting from the wide end where the air pocket lives.

Edge cases. Timing assumes the water stays at a lively boil and the eggs are not stacked three deep. Cooking more than six at once? Give the water 30 seconds to recover after the eggs go in.
