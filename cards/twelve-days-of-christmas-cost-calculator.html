Pascal's Triangle, triangular numbers (T₁₂ = 78) & tetrahedral numbers (Te₁₂ = 364)

Two classic sequences in one carol: On Day k alone, your True Love sends the k-th triangular number of gifts: T_k = 1 + 2 + … + k = k(k + 1) / 2, so Day 12 brings T₁₂ = (12 × 13) / 2 = 78 items on that final morning. Summing across all N = 12 mornings yields the 12th tetrahedral number (the 4th diagonal of Pascal's Triangle!): Te_N = N(N + 1)(N + 2) / 6 = (12 × 13 × 14) / 6 = 364 gifts — remarkably, one gift for every day of a standard year except Christmas Day itself!

Why Geese and Swans tie at 42: The gift introduced in verse d arrives in batches of d on (N + 1 − d) consecutive mornings, giving Q(d) = d(13 − d) = 13d − d². Because the vertex of 13d − d² sits at d = 6.5, integer maxima occur symmetrically at d = 6 (Six Geese × 7 days = 42) and d = 7 (Seven Swans × 6 days = 42), while 12 Partridges and 12 Drummers tie at the outer ends! Note also that "five gold rings" likely referred originally to five ring-necked pheasants — which would raise the bird count from 184 to 224!
