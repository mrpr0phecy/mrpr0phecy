❄️ Snowflake Growth Chamber

Nakaya's diagram in a dish. Temperature and supersaturation decide whether a crystal becomes a plate, a needle, a sector or a dendrite — then a hexagonal cellular automaton grows that habit from a single seed. No two flakes are alike because the vapour field is never quite symmetric.

Dendrites like −15 °C and moist air. Needles like −5 °C.
The physics

Ukichiro Nakaya mapped habit against temperature and supersaturation in the 1930s: plates near 0 °C and −10 to −22 °C, columns and needles near −5 °C, stellar dendrites near −15 °C in moist air. Growth is diffusion-limited on a hexagonal lattice — tips with one ice neighbour win when supersaturation is high (dendrites); filling-in wins when it is low (plates). Six-fold symmetry is the ice Ih crystal; the uniqueness is noise in the vapour field.
