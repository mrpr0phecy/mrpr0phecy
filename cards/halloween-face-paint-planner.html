      How the timings, paint quantities and skin-tone notes are worked out

      How long does Halloween face paint take?

      Each design is broken into steps with a realistic minute figure — a full skeleton is about 20 minutes on one face, a cat about 16. Half-face coverage uses 60% of the step time because the base coat is skipped and the edges are simpler. Add 3 minutes per face for setting out and sponges.

      How much face paint do I need?

      The estimate assumes 280 cm² painted for a half face and 560 cm² for a full one, 1.4 coats, and 0.012 ml of paint per cm² per coat. A full face therefore uses about 9 ml, so a 30 ml pot covers roughly three faces. Buy small pots of the three or four colours the design actually uses rather than a 12-colour kit you will not finish.

      Does skin tone change the design?

      Yes, and the simplest change is not to paint the whole face. On fair and medium skin a white or ivory base gives the bone look. On deep skin, painting a full white base flattens the face and cracks at the edges; instead paint only the bone lines and eye sockets in white, and leave the skin itself as the deepest shadow. The tool switches the note and the base colour accordingly.

      Is Halloween face paint safe for children?

      Use cosmetic-grade face paint labelled for skin, patch-test on the inner arm 24 hours before, and keep paint out of the eyes and mouth. Never use acrylic or poster paint — it is not made for skin and can stain or irritate. Stop and wash off if the skin stings, and take it off before bed with a fragrance-free oil-based cleanser rather than scrubbing.

      How long does it take to dry?

      Water-based face paint dries in about 4 minutes per coat, so 6–8 minutes for a base plus detail. Powder over the top sets it, and a setting spray adds about 20 minutes of smudge resistance. Put the wig, hood or hat on after drying, or it will drag the paint off the hairline.
