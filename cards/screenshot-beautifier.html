How it works, redaction that actually hides things, and tips

Getting the image in. Take a screenshot as usual (Win+Shift+S, ⌘⇧4, or the Print Screen key), then come here and press Ctrl+V or ⌘V — the image is read straight from your clipboard. You can also drop a file onto the tool or choose one. Nothing is uploaded: the picture is drawn onto a canvas in your own browser and the PNG you download is made there too. Screenshots larger than 4,096 px on a side are scaled down to that limit so older phones do not run out of memory.

Padding, corners and shadow are percentages of the screenshot's size, so the same settings look right on a tiny mobile capture and a 4K desktop. "Canvas" pads the result out to a fixed shape — 16:9 for X and LinkedIn, 4:5 or 1:1 for Instagram, 9:16 for stories — with the screenshot centred, so a tall or wide capture posts without being cropped by the platform. Export at 1× keeps every pixel of the original; 2× doubles the output so the frame, shadow and rounded corners stay crisp on high-density screens (it does not add detail to the screenshot itself).

Redaction you can trust. Press Redact, then drag boxes over anything private — email addresses, names, keys, balances. The boxes are solid, flat colour, baked into the pixels of the exported image. That matters: blurred or pixelated text has been recovered many times, because a blur is a reversible smear of the original pixels. A solid box contains none of them. Boxes only cover the screenshot itself, not the frame, and Undo box removes the last one. Also remember that the window frame's title text is yours to write: the original tab title is not read from the image.

Clean metadata. The exported PNG is redrawn from scratch, so any EXIF, location or device metadata in the original file is not carried across. The file name is the original name with "-beautified" added.

Copy vs download. Copy image puts the PNG straight on your clipboard for pasting into Slack, a doc or a post; it needs a recent Chrome, Edge or Safari and a secure page. Firefox may refuse the copy — the download works everywhere. Your settings (not your images) are remembered on this device.
