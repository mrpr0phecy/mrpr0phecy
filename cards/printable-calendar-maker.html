Holiday rules, week numbers, events and printing tips

Holidays are worked out from the rules, not looked up. For England and Wales that means New Year’s Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, the early May and spring bank holidays, the summer bank holiday, Christmas Day and Boxing Day, with substitute days when a holiday falls at the weekend. Scotland swaps Easter Monday for 2 January, takes its summer holiday on the first Monday of August and adds St Andrew’s Day; Northern Ireland adds St Patrick’s Day and the Twelfth of July. The US federal list is the eleven holidays with their observed days. Easter is computed with the standard Gregorian method, so any year from 1900 to 2199 works. One-off holidays announced by governments — a coronation, a jubilee, a day of mourning — cannot be predicted by a rule, so add those as events yourself.

Week numbers follow ISO 8601: week 1 is the week containing the first Thursday of the year, and weeks run Monday to Sunday. The numbers are shown in a narrow column on the left and are exact whichever day you choose to start the printed week on.

Events are one per line. A date without a year repeats every year, so a birthdays list written once works for every calendar you print. Accepted forms are day/month (14/03, British order), an ISO date (2026-03-14), or a day and month name in either order (14 Mar, March 14). Up to three lines fit in a day square; longer text is shortened to fit. Everything you type stays in this browser and is remembered for next time.

Printing. The PDF is drawn at 150 dots per inch, one page per month (or a single year page), and opens in any PDF viewer. Print at 100% with the paper size you picked so the grid uses the whole sheet. “Notes lines” adds ruled space under the grid for shopping lists and reminders. The accent colour is used for the title, holidays and today’s highlight only, so the calendar stays cheap to print.
