Why these prices, plainly

Comparable services charge roughly £60–£145 a year for eight mortgage calculators, or $79 a month for around eighteen. Those are real published prices, and they're the market I'm pricing against. The category tier here is 35 tools for £299 — cheaper per tool by a wide margin, because I'm not carrying a sales team, a CRM integration, or a venture-backed cost base.

What I'm not going to do is pretend that makes them better at everything. Those platforms offer lead capture, CRM sync and analytics dashboards. Mine don't, and won't — the tools don't collect visitor data, which is the whole point of them. If your reason for wanting a calculator is harvesting leads from it, buy one of theirs; you'll be happier.

What you're actually buying

Not the HTML — you could write a mortgage calculator yourself in an afternoon. What's being sold is the maintenance, and that is the part people underestimate:

  • The UK statutory figures move every April. Income tax bands, National Insurance rates and thresholds, student loan plan thresholds, the personal allowance taper. A calculator with last year's numbers is worse than no calculator, because it's confidently wrong.
  • The maths is genuinely easy to get wrong. The personal allowance taper above £100,000 creates a 60% marginal rate that most calculators — including some commercial ones — miss entirely, because they model the basic rate band as a fixed ceiling rather than a fixed width. These ones handle it, along with Scottish bands.
  • It's checked automatically. Every deploy runs a suite of financial assertions against the calculators. If a change breaks a tax computation, the build fails before it ships.

That is the recurring value, and it's why this is priced annually rather than as a one-off. You're renting correctness.
