Drone Nearby? A Calm Safety & Reporting Guide

Drone Nearby? A Calm Safety & Reporting Guide

Choose what you have actually observed to get a civilian-safe next-step checklist. This guide cannot identify a drone, its operator or intent—and a sighting alone is not evidence of a threat or an offence.

Do not approach, chase, touch, seize, jam or try to disable a drone or its debris. Do not confront the operator or send another aircraft to investigate. Move away from immediate danger, follow official instructions and report observable facts through an appropriate local channel.

No location or personal details are requested or saved.

Official guidance and important limits

For people in the UK, the Civil Aviation Authority says many drone flights are allowed and a sighting alone does not show that a flight is unlawful. Its current public guidance directs concerns about dangerous or illegal flying to local police on 101; call 999 for immediate danger to life or a threat of violence. Rules and reporting routes differ elsewhere—use your local police, aviation authority and emergency number.

This is general preparedness information, not a live alert, threat assessment, legal opinion or instruction to approach an aircraft. It cannot confirm whether a report is genuine. Share only verified information with the relevant authority; avoid posting unverified claims, identifiable people or precise real-time details publicly.

Sources: UK CAA: concerns about safety, privacy and illegal flying · UK CAA: serious incidents and near misses.
