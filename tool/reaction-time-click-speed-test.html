⚡ Reaction Time, Click Speed & Aim Test

⚡ Reaction Time, Click Speed & Aim Test

Three quick reflex benchmarks in one place: how fast you react to a colour change (in milliseconds), how many clicks per second you can manage, and how accurately you hit moving targets. Personal bests are kept on this device.

Click or press space to start
Wait for the box to turn green, then click as fast as you can.
How the numbers are measured, what is typical, and what adds latency

Reaction time. After a random delay of 1.5–5 seconds the box turns green and a high-resolution timestamp is taken; your click or key press takes a second one and the difference is the trial time. Clicking early voids that trial. The score is the mean of your trials — the median is also shown because one lapse should not define you. Typical results for a simple visual reaction task are around 200–300 ms, with a median for online testers near 270 ms; elite sprinters are held to a 100 ms minimum because anything faster is treated as anticipation.

Latency you cannot remove. Your monitor refreshes 60–240 times a second, so on a 60 Hz screen the green frame appears on average 8 ms after the timestamp; a wireless mouse and the browser's input pipeline add a few more. That means every score here is a little slower than your nervous system — but consistently so, which is what makes repeated tests comparable.

Clicks per second. Every pointer-down inside the box counts, from the first click until the timer runs out. 6–8 CPS is a normal fast click; jitter and butterfly clicking techniques reach 10–15. Touch screens count taps. Auto-clickers are, of course, cheating yourself.

Aim. One target at a time for 30 seconds; a click that misses still counts as a shot, so accuracy is hits ÷ shots. Targets never spawn under the cursor and are kept at least one diameter from the edge.

Storage. Personal bests are saved in this browser only. This is a game and a rough benchmark, not a medical or occupational assessment; tiredness, caffeine and screen size all move the numbers.
