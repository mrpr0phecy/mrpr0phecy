🌀 The Coriolis Effect — Turntable & Storms

🌀 The Coriolis Effect — Turntable & Storms

Nothing "curves" in the air — but on a rotating planet, straight motion looks curved. Throw a ball across a spinning disk, then watch the same physics organise hurricanes: right-deflected in the north, left in the south, absent at the equator.

📖 Key facts & a famous myth

Earth turns once a day, so any object moving over the ground — artillery shells, long-range rockets, weather systems — is deflected right of its motion in the Northern Hemisphere, left in the Southern, with zero effect at the equator growing to maximum at the poles. The ball on the disk flies perfectly straight through the air; the curve you see is the disk rotating underneath — exactly how "Coriolis force" is a consequence of choosing a rotating viewpoint.

For storms the deflection is everything: air rushing toward a low-pressure centre gets turned aside and settles into rotation — counterclockwise in the north, clockwise in the south. Coriolis is why hurricanes can't form within ~5° of the equator and why trade winds blow steadily from the east. The sink myth: your bathtub's whirlpool direction is set by its shape and leftover motion — the Coriolis effect is millions of times too weak at sink scale.
