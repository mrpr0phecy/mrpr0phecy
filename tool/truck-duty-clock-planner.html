Where these numbers come from — and where they can bite

US: 49 CFR 395.3 — 11 hours driving, 14-hour on-duty window, 30-minute break after 8 cumulative hours of driving, 10 consecutive hours off to reset, and 60 hours in 7 days or 70 in 8 with a 34-hour restart. Split sleeper berth pairings (8+2 or 7+3, neither period under 2 hours) pause the 14-hour clock — 395.1(g). Drivers inside the 150 air-mile short-haul exception run on 395.1(e) rules instead, which this planner does not model.

Canada: SOR/2005-313 — 13 hours driving, 14 hours on duty (16 with a deferral), 8 consecutive hours off before a shift, 10 hours off in the day, 70 hours in 7 days or 120 in 14. There is no federal break requirement; some provinces add one.

EU / GB: Regulation (EC) 561/2006 — 9 hours daily driving, extendable to 10 twice in a week; a 45-minute break (splittable 15 + 30) after 4.5 hours of driving; 11 hours daily rest, reducible to 9 three times between weekly rests; 56 hours in a week and 90 in any two consecutive weeks. GB domestic rules for goods vehicles are separate and stricter on the working day — check which regime your work falls under.

Australia: NHVR Heavy Vehicle Fatigue Rules, standard hours for a solo driver — maximum 12 hours work in any 24, at least 30 continuous minutes of rest after 5.5 hours of work, 7 continuous hours stationary in 24, and 24 continuous hours of rest in 7 days. Basic Fatigue Management and AFM change all of these numbers; this models standard hours only.

Rules change, exemptions exist and enforcement varies. This is a planning aid built from the published limits, not a legal opinion or a substitute for your log. When the number here matters, read the rule text that applies to you.
