      How the games are chosen and the running order is timed

      How many party games do I need for a two-hour party?

      About 100 minutes of games for a 120-minute party: 12 minutes per game including the changeover, or roughly eight games. Children under five need shorter games and more of them — five minutes each, with a craft in the middle — while teenagers will happily play three long ones. The planner stops selecting once the running time plus a 10-minute buffer reaches your party length.

      Why does the order alternate high and low energy?

      Because a room of children cannot sprint for two hours. The planner starts with one active game to burn off arrival energy, then alternates an active game with a calm one, and ends on something quiet so the sugar and the shouting wind down before pickup. Choose "high" if you really do want a running party, and it will pick active games first.

      What changes with the space?

      Garden games get the water, the running and the mess; a living room rules out anything that needs a 10-metre run, so apple bobbing, relays and tag drop out and crafts, trivia and doughnuts come in. A hall sits in between and can take the running games if you move the chairs back.

      Which games are not suitable for very young children?

      Anything with small parts (doughnut strings, beads, slime borax), anything with water and shared basins, and anything that involves blindfolds or chasing in a tight space. The age filter drops those automatically; for a mixed group, plan two tracks — a craft for the little ones while the older ones run the relay.

      What about the sweets?

      Keep the sweet table away from the games and hand out a small bag at the end. A "guess the candy count" jar earns its place as the quiet final game, and the candy calculator works out how much to buy for the headcount.
