Where these numbers come from, and when not to trust them

Cup measures use the metric cup of 250 ml used in the UK, Australia and New Zealand. A US cup is 240 ml, so a US recipe's cup is about 4% smaller — rarely decisive in a stew, occasionally decisive in a sponge. Weight-to-volume figures are typical packed values for the ingredient as you would spoon and level it; flour scooped straight from the bag and compressed can weigh 10–20% more, which is exactly why serious baking recipes give grams.

Fan ovens circulate air and cook hotter for the same dial setting, so the usual rule is 20 °C lower than the conventional figure in the recipe. Gas marks are the standard UK scale (mark 4 = 180 °C = 350 °F). Home ovens vary by 10–20 °C from their dial, so an oven thermometer beats any conversion table.

Substitutions are for cooking, not for allergies or intolerances: swapping one dairy or gluten product for another does not make a dish safe. Check the label. Nothing here leaves your device — no network request is made.
