🔧 Repair or Replace?

🔧 Repair or Replace?

The throwaway economy wants you to bin it and buy another. This decider weighs the three things that actually matter — cost per useful year, embodied carbon and remaining life — and tells you honestly: repair it, replace it, or too close to call. Everything runs on this device; nothing is sent anywhere.

🔩 Keeping the old one alive (right-to-repair)
  • Since 2021, UK/EU rules force makers of many appliances to sell spare parts for 7–10 years — you can ask for the part and fit it yourself.
  • Free fix help: iFixit guides, Repair Café volunteers, and your local restart party.
  • 80% of appliance faults are limescale, blocked filters, worn door seals or brushes — all cheap.
  • Get the repair in writing; many engineers guarantee the work for 12 months.

Method: money compared as £ per useful year (repair + running costs ÷ years bought, vs price + running ÷ lifespan), plus the tradesperson's 50% rule. Carbon compares a repair (parts ≈ 3% of a new unit's footprint + ~5 kg for the engineer's van) against making a whole new one plus its running emissions. Embodied-carbon presets are rough mid-points of published lifecycle studies and vary by model; grid intensity defaults to ~0.20 kg CO₂/kWh (UK, 2025) — adjust both above. Estimates for information only, not financial or environmental advice. Last reviewed: 2026-09-23.
