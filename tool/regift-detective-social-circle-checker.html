    The ethics & social-graph mathematics of sustainable regifting

    Why thoughtful regifting is genuinely good: Passing an unopened luxury candle, bottle of wine or unread book to someone who will actually treasure it beats letting it gather dust in a hallway cupboard for four years. Etiquette researchers distinguish thoughtful re-homing (brand-new, factory-sealed, matched to the recipient's taste, zero social overlap with the original giver) from boomerang dumping (offloading unwanted clutter inside the same social graph).

    The 3 cardinal rules that prevent 99% of disasters: (1) Never regift when provenance is unknown — if you cannot name who gave you the item, Murphy's Law guarantees you are handing it straight back to them. (2) Shake out every outer gift bag and box — small folded "To/From" tags routinely slip beneath cardboard inserts. (3) Pair with a fresh intentional companion (e.g., tie fresh artisan biscuits or a handwritten note onto a regifted bottle or candle) so the gift feels curated rather than excavated.
