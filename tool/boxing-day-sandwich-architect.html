    The culinary physics of moisture barriers, friction & leftover turkey safety

    Why bread turns soggy in 90 seconds without a lipid barrier

    Warm gravy and cranberry sauce are aqueous liquids that wick into bare starch via capillary action. Spreading salted butter, mayonnaise or Brie directly onto toasted outer slices creates a hydrophobic fat barrier, trapping moisture inside the centre Moist Maker slice while keeping the outer crust crisp.

    Why halved pigs in blankets prevent "filling avalanche"

    Whole cylindrical sausages roll like ball bearings when you bite down, ejecting turkey out the back of the sandwich. Slicing pigs in blankets lengthwise flat-side-down locks them into the stuffing/cranberry mortar.

    Food safety rules for cooked Christmas meats

    UK Food Standards Agency (FSA) guidance: cool cooked turkey within 1–2 hours, store covered in a fridge at ≤5 °C, and eat cold leftovers within 2 days (up to 3–4 days per USDA at ≤4 °C; FSA advises 48 hours for cooked poultry/rice, or freeze promptly). If reheating gravy, stuffing or meat, heat only once until steaming hot throughout (75 °C).
