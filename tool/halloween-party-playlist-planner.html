    How the blocks, the songs and the runtime are chosen

    How many songs do I need for a Halloween party?

    About 15 songs an hour, because a party playlist averages three to four minutes a track. A two-hour party is around 30 songs, and this planner fills each block to the minute rather than guessing. Songs are shuffled within their block, so the same seed always gives the same running order.

    Why split the night into blocks?

    Because the same song is a different song at 7 pm and 10 pm. The planner builds five blocks — arrival, games, food, dancing and wind-down — and aims each at an energy level: quiet and eerie while people arrive, louder through the games, quieter while everyone eats, loudest for the dancing, then down again so the last guests leave rather than cling on.

    What is safe for children?

    Choosing "mostly children" filters out the adult tracks and keeps the cartoon-adjacent and pop songs: Monster Mash, Ghostbusters, This Is Halloween, Spooky Scary Skeletons, the Addams Family theme. The lyrics in that set are mild; the loudest tracks are still kid-appropriate. Family mixes allow both sets.

    How do I keep the music going between songs?

    Use a crossfade of three to five seconds and put the playlist in a single queue with no gaps. The runtime tile shows how far the chosen songs fill the party; if there is a gap, add a second pass through the wind-down block rather than repeating the whole list.

    Are the runtimes exact?

    No — they are approximate album lengths, which is what you need for planning. A single edit or a live version can be a minute shorter or longer, so keep 10% of slack in the queue rather than timing the cake to the second.
