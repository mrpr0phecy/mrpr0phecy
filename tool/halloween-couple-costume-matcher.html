    How the pairs are matched, costed and timed

    What makes a good couple costume?

    Two costumes that read instantly as a pair, but still work if you are separated in a crowd. That means one strong silhouette each, a shared colour or prop, and a name people can shout across a room. The list here is built on that rule rather than on matching outfits that only make sense side by side.

    How are the costs worked out?

    Each pair carries a realistic total for two people, assuming charity-shop and existing wardrobe first: under £20 means a DIY or two cheap accessories, £20–60 is a costume each or one good prop, £60+ assumes bought outfits. The figure excludes shoes you already own and makeup you will use again.

    How long does a DIY pair take?

    Low is under two hours with glue, tape and a printer. Medium is an evening — a painted prop, a modified shirt, a wig styled. High is a weekend: sewing, foam armour, a papier-mâché head. The chart plots the hours the pair actually takes, not the effort band, so a "medium" that needs six hours shows as six.

    What if one of us hates dressing up?

    Pick an asymmetric pair where one costume is almost normal clothes: vampire and victim, Wednesday and Thing (a hand on a box), Han and Leia, Bob Ross and a painting. The tool filters these to the top when you choose low effort, and the "who it suits" tile calls out the lopsided ones.

    Can we reuse these next year?

    Most of the low and medium pairs are built from clothes rather than bought pieces, so yes. Store wigs on a stand or a stuffed bag, keep face paint off fabric, and photograph the finished look — reassembling it next October is much faster with a reference shot.

    Last reviewed: 2026-10-20. Costs are UK planning figures for two adults and will vary by what is already in the wardrobe.
