🚭 Smoking Cost & Quit Savings Planner

🚭 Smoking Cost & Quit Savings Planner

A pack a day is a used car every year. Set what you actually smoke, pick a quit date, and watch the money timeline — plus the recovery milestones your body hits along the way.

Rolling tobacco, vapes and nights out are separate budgets — this counts cigarettes only. Prices here are editable placeholders; use what you actually pay.
Method, the recovery milestones, and getting free help

Method. Daily spend = (cigarettes ÷ pack size) × pack price. Everything scales from that one number: spent-to-date assumes the same rate for the whole period, paused at the quit date. It is arithmetic, not a prediction — price rises tend to make the future column flattering.

What your body does next (NHS-published timeline): after ~20 minutes heart rate and blood pressure drop; at 8 hours carbon monoxide in the blood halves; at 48 hours carbon monoxide is gone and taste and smell sharpen; within 2–12 weeks circulation improves; by 3–9 months coughing and wheeze ease as lung function rises; at 1 year the elevated heart-attack risk halves; at 10 years the added lung-cancer risk halves compared with someone who kept smoking.

Free help doubles the odds. In England, local NHS stop-smoking services are free and offer varenicline, patches and coaching; you are several times more likely to quit for good with support than willpower alone. The NHS Smokefree app tracks the same savings this calculator shows.

Edge cases. Weeks are fuzzy during a taper — entering half a cigarette per day works. The “spent so far” number ignores inflation and any earlier heavier periods; treat it as a floor.

Sources: arithmetic modelling plus the NHS stop-smoking recovery timeline. Not medical advice — your GP or pharmacist can prescribe stop-smoking support. Last reviewed 2026-09-21.
