### On D-002 — I was non-compliant and have fixed it

Before finding this folder I audited privacy claims independently and reached a
*weaker* answer than D-002: I scoped the claims ("no tracking **in any tool**")
rather than removing them, on the evidence that 3 top-level pages ran GA and all
562 cards were clean.

That was wrong on the facts and wrong on the principle:

1. **Wrong on the facts.** `01a062bc` loads `analytics.js` on **45** top-level
   pages, not 3. My scoped wording would have become misleading the moment that
   merged.
2. **Wrong on the principle.** A scoped claim needs a reader to parse the scope
   correctly. D-002's blanket ban has nothing to get wrong — it's the more
   robust rule, and I've replaced my check with it.

My own check also **missed** `100% Private` in the index.html hero badge and
three unqualified claims on `tool.html`. D-002's explicit phrase list caught
them. Good decision; I've adopted it wholesale.

## What I added to the shared toolkit

`scripts/check-finance.js` now enforces, for everyone:

- **D-001:** every advertised tool count must equal the real number of cards on
  disk. Strips inline tags first, so `all <strong>500</strong> tools` is caught
  — my first version missed exactly that markup.
- **D-002:** no page may contain "no tracking", "no trackers", "no analytics",
  "no cookies" or a blanket "100% private". Scoped in-browser processing claims
  are allowed, because the cards genuinely make zero network calls (verified).
- **No analytics in any tool card** — this is what keeps "runs in your browser"
  true.
- **No placebo controls** — after finding Snowball/Avalanche buttons on
  `debtpayoff.html` that changed a caption and produced identical numbers.
- **Sponsorship 5% rule** — one labelled slot per page max (owner's constraint).

Every guard was deliberately broken before committing, to confirm it fails.
One of them immediately caught a real instance I'd missed by hand.
